## The goal

The owner wants two people in her day: the ranger's partner, who keeps
turning up, and Hal, who tells her how to get the gondola running and dies
once she has his key. Neither should look like anybody in RE1. The plan for
doing that:
- take an existing RE1 character's model, with its mesh, skeleton and
  animations;
- give each a new skin: a texture we paint ourselves, with small mesh changes
  only if the skin can't carry the look alone.

Done means:

1. **A pipeline.**
   - A tool reads an RE1 `.EMD` model from the player's own game files.
   - A Blender script shows it on its skeleton with its texture.
   - A tool writes the model back out with our texture in place of the original's.
   - A test proves the round trip: writing a model unchanged gives the same bytes.
2. **Their skins.** Painted by us at the texture's own size, palette and layout.
   No pixels are copied from the game's art.
3. **Turnaround renders.** Front, side and back, plus one in the game's
   lighting, published for the owner at `https://home.arpa/research/rebecca-npc/`.
4. **In the game.** Each stands in a room in her mode (`--mode REB`), plays an
   idle, and is screenshotted.
5. **The rules hold.**
   - The new models reach the game only as a patch built from the player's
     own model files and our textures.
   - Chris's and Jill's games are unchanged: her overlay supplies it, in her mode only.

## A suggested shape

1. `tools/rebecca/npc/emd.ts` reads and writes an `.EMD`. It splits out the
   texture, meshes, skeleton and animations, keeps everything else as bytes,
   and writes it back. Test it with `tools/rebecca/npc/emd.test.ts` against
   the player's own files: a round trip unchanged, and a swapped texture read
   back.
2. `tools/rebecca/npc/view.py` is a Blender script. It builds the mesh on its
   skeleton in its rest pose, or in an animation frame, with the texture, and
   renders front, side and back.
3. The skin. Paint it in the texture's UV layout. For example, render the UV
   islands from Blender as a guide, then paint over them in Blender's texture
   paint, or generate the texture with procedural Blender materials baked to
   the UVs. Fit it to the texture's size and bit depth.
4. `tools/rebecca/build.ts` gets a step that writes their models into the REB
   overlay from the player's own file plus our skin. You may add that step;
   the Claude session won't touch `build.ts` while your PR is open.
5. For the in-game proof, place them in the cabin. Its script is
   `tools/rebecca/day/lookout/room.c`, hand-written, and the Claude session
   doesn't touch it. Then screenshot them through the test link.

## Blender, on jellybingus, sharing one GPU

- Run Blender on jellybingus only: `"C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.1\blender.exe" -b --factory-startup --python <script>`.
  Use a work folder of your own, such as
  `C:\Users\joell\Documents\Blender\re1-npc\`.
- **Every Blender run takes a GPU slot first.** The Claude session is
  re-rendering 51 rooms on the same GPU. Seventeen renders at once once
  filled its 24 GB and froze them all for seven hours. Wrap each run:

  ```sh
  node tools/rebecca/day/slots.ts npc -- ssh -o BatchMode=yes jellybingus 'cmd /c "...blender.exe -b ..."'
  ```

  From TypeScript, use `withSlot('npc', () => ...)` from
  `tools/rebecca/day/slots.ts`. It waits its turn and frees the slot however
  the run ends.
- Keep previews small and the sample counts low until the look is settled.
- Never stop a Blender process you didn't start. Find yours by its command
  line:

  ```powershell
  Get-CimInstance Win32_Process | Where-Object { $_.Name -eq 'blender.exe' -and $_.CommandLine -like '*re1-npc*' }
  ```
- Don't write queue scripts or loops that outlive you.
